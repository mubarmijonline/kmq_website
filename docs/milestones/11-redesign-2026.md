# 11 — 2026 redesign

**Goal.** The client's 2026 brief: a new colour system, a hero that plays on
its own instead of lagging on scroll, real branch photography, and photographs
on the service cards. A redesign, not a rewrite — the copy in both languages
comes out byte-identical apart from newly written alt text.

**Includes**

- **Colour.** `design/tokens.css` becomes two layers: the brief's palette
  transcribed once as literals, and the older `--kmq-*` names re-pointed at it.
  Nothing in `components.css` carries a colour of its own any more, including
  the `rgba()` triples that a hex grep does not find. Documented exceptions:
  WhatsApp's brand green, which is theirs, and the `#000` stops inside mask
  gradients, which are luminance rather than colour.
- **Type.** Self-hosted woff2, subset per script, generated by
  `scripts/fetch_fonts.py`. Arabic keeps Tajawal and Almarai; English gains
  Inter for body under Space Grotesk. Two preloads per locale, no
  render-blocking font request. Latin display gets negative tracking, Arabic
  never does.
- **Rhythm.** Dark hero, dark trust, one light `--paper-50` services section,
  then dark to the footer. `.kmq-section--paper` redefines the variables the
  dark sections use rather than adding a light variant of every component.
- **Hero.** Four photographs of one car crossfading on a timer, replacing the
  scroll-driven coating build-up. See milestone notes below.
- **Photography.** Five branch storefronts, five service photographs, all
  emitted as AVIF/WebP with a fallback at four widths by
  `scripts/build_images.py`.
- **Measurement.** `scripts/audit.py` — Lighthouse mobile (median of three),
  section screenshots at three widths in both locales, a 4x-CPU-throttled
  recording of the hero, and a contrast and focus pass over the rendered page.

**Why the old hero was slow.** Not the things that usually do it. The scroll
listener was already rAF-gated and passive, the images came to 160 KB, there
was no canvas and no scroll-jacking. The cost was in what the `clip-path` was
clipping: eight copies of one PNG carrying stacked drop-shadows,
`mix-blend-mode: screen`, `mask-composite: intersect`, four- and five-stage
filter chains and a rescaled PNG mask — none of it compositable, so every
frame re-rastered on the main thread, under an 18px `backdrop-filter` header
that re-sampled the result. The progress bar animated `width`, and the next
frame read `getBoundingClientRect`, forcing a synchronous layout each tick.

**Acceptance.**

- Lighthouse mobile Performance ≥ 90, LCP ≤ 2.5s, CLS ≤ 0.05, TBT ≤ 200ms,
  measured as a median of three against a gzipped origin.
- The hero starts within 1s of load and holds its cycle with no long task
  attributable to the animation on a 4x-throttled CPU.
- No scroll listener drives animation anywhere. One remains, for the header's
  opacity, and it drives an attribute rather than a frame.
- Every colour in `design/` resolves to a token bar the documented exceptions.
- `/ar/` renders RTL at 360, 768 and 1440 with no horizontal overflow and no
  mirrored photograph or logo.
- Zero contrast failures over the rendered page, both locales; every tab stop
  paints a visible focus ring.
- Reduced motion: the hero does not auto-cycle and the rail still switches.
- Copy byte-identical in both languages, except alt text, which is new.

**Status.** Delivered. Numbers in `docs/audit/`; regenerate with
`python3 scripts/audit.py <label>`.

**Open.**

- Five branch photographs for six branches. Al-Manar keeps the shared entrance
  shot until a sixth arrives; the gap is recorded in `_BRANCH_PHOTOS`.
- `t.stack` — the bare-paint-to-ceramic narrative the old hero told — is still
  in `app/content.py` and no longer rendered anywhere. Delete or rehome.
- The site has no sitemap.xml, robots.txt, canonical link, OG or Twitter tags
  and no structured data. Out of scope here, and worth its own milestone.
